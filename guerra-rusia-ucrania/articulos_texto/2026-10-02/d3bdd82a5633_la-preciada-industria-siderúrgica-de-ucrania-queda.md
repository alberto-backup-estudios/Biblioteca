## Noticia Completa Traducida al Español:

Como miles de otros residentes de Zaporizhzhia, Denys Maksyshko ha pasado años trabajando para la fábrica local de acero Zaporizhstal. Durante generaciones, ha sido la fuente de empleo más grande y rica en esta importante ciudad ucraniana.

"Zaporizhstal no es sólo una gran empresa, es el corazón de la ciudad", afirma.

Ahora, tras una serie de ataques con misiles rusos, este corazón ha dejado de latir. La enorme fábrica que emplea a 8.500 personas cerró indefinidamente después de cuatro oleadas de ataques rusos: dos en agosto y dos en septiembre.

"Todo el equipamiento ha sido destruido casi por completo. No quedan ventanas y los tejados tienen agujeros. Los altos hornos han quedado casi completamente destruidos", se queja Maksyshko.

Rusia disparó un total de 17 misiles contra Zaporizhstal, según Oleksandr Myronenko, director de operaciones del propietario de la acería Metinvest. Ocho trabajadores murieron y 31 resultaron heridos.

"Ahora estamos limpiando escombros y tratando de planificar la recuperación", le dice Myronenko a la BBC.

## Texto Original (EN):

### Ukraine's prized steel industry left in ruins by Russian missile campaign

Like thousands of other residents of Zaporizhzhia, Denys Maksyshko has spent years working for local steel factory Zaporizhstal. For generations, it has been the biggest and richest source of employment in this major Ukrainian city.

"Zaporizhstal is not just a big company, it's the heart of the city," he says.

Now, following a series of Russian missile strikes, this heart has stopped beating. The huge factory that employs 8,500 people shut down indefinitely after four waves of Russian attacks - two in August, and two in September.

"All the equipment has been almost completely destroyed. There are no windows left, and there are holes in the roofs. The blast furnaces have been almost completely ruined," Maksyshko complains.

Russia fired a total of 17 missiles at Zaporizhstal, according to Oleksandr Myronenko, chief operating officer of steelworks owner Metinvest. Eight workers were killed and 31 wounded.

"We're now clearing rubble and trying to plan recovery," Myronenko tells the BBC.
