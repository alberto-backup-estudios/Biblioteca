## Noticia Completa Traducida al Español:

A principios de junio de 2026, justo cuando estaba en marcha la Forma Económica Internacional de San Petersburgo, Ucrania atacó la ciudad con drones de largo alcance. Ese ataque marcó efectivamente el comienzo de una nueva fase de la guerra, descrita en los círculos del gobierno de Kiev como un intento de obligar a Rusia a entablar conversaciones de paz.

La doble estrategia de ataques con drones y cartas abiertas, que Ukrainska Pravda cubrió en detalle, consistió en atacar la infraestructura crítica de Rusia, por un lado, aumentar constantemente el alcance de los ataques a 1.000-3.000 kilómetros y, por el otro, ofrecer iniciar conversaciones de paz en cualquier momento.

La expectativa general era que en la segunda mitad del verano las fuerzas de defensa de Ucrania lanzarían un bloqueo con aviones no tripulados de Crimea y el sur de Ucrania, dejando el Mar de Azov y el Mar Negro completamente inutilizables para el transporte marítimo ruso. Se suponía que esto tendría lugar en sincronía con una mayor presión de sanciones por parte de los socios de Ucrania para que en otoño, después de las elecciones rusas, pudieran presionar conjuntamente a los rusos para que entablaran negociaciones genuinas.

## Texto Original (EN):

### Donnyland abandoned: the last negotiations of the year

In early June 2026, just as the St Petersburg International Economic Form was underway, Ukraine attacked the city with long-range drones. That attack effectively marked the beginning of a new phase of the war, described in Kyiv government circles as an attempt to force Russia into peace talks.

The two-pronged strategy of drone attacks and open letters, which Ukrainska Pravda covered in detail, consisted of striking Russia's critical infrastructure on the one hand, steadily increasing the range of attacks to 1,000-3,000 km, and offering to begin peace talks at any time on the other.

The general expectation was that in the second half of the summer Ukraine's defence forces would launch a drone blockade of Crimea and Ukraine's south, making the Sea of Azov and the Black Sea completely unusable for Russian shipping. This was supposed to take place in sync with increased sanctions pressure from Ukraine's partners so that in the autumn, after the Russian elections, they could jointly push the Russians to genuinely engage in negotiations.

Back in June, a source in President Zelenskyy's diplomatic team explained the logic behind Ukraine's actions to UP: "Taking together all the military, diplomatic and international factors, a certain window of opportunity for negotiations may open in October or November – sometime before the US elections and after the Russian  State Duma
elections."

As of early autumn, the Armed Forces of Ukraine have achieved most of their military objectives. In some respects – such as disrupting Russian logistics in the south and targeting the shadow fleet – they have even surpassed expectations.

Yet it cannot be said that Ukraine has moved any closer to achieving its main objective – the start of substantive negotiations. Quite the opposite.
