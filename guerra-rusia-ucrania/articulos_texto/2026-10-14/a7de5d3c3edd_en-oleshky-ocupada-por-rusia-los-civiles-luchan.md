## Texto Original (EN):

### In Russian-Occupied Oleshky, Civilians Struggle to Survive as Hunger Deepens

Lyudmila often has to call her sister about 40 times before the line connects. When she finally gets through, she can barely make out what her sister is saying.

"I hear occasional words, sometimes they're incomplete. The main thing is that I hear her voice," Lyudmila, whose name has been changed for safety reasons, told The Moscow Times.

A refugee now living in Europe, Lyudmila comes from a village on the Ukrainian-controlled right bank of the Dnipro River in the Kherson region. Her sister lives across the river in the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky, where relentless bombardment has plunged the town into a deepening humanitarian crisis.

Conditions in Oleshky have continued to deteriorate since late 2025. Roads leading in and out of the town are heavily mined and badly damaged, leaving its 2,000 remaining residents, including an estimated 50 children, largely cut off from food, water, gas, electricity and medicine, residents and volunteers told The Moscow Times.

"She says everything is destroyed, houses are often burning and strikes land nearby every five to 10 minutes," Lyudmila told The Moscow Times.

With a pre-war population of 24,000, Oleshky served as a satellite town to Kherson city over the Dnipro River. It became a frontline town after Russian forces retreated across the Dnipro in late 2022.
